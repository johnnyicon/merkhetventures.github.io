# Merkhet Ventures — Content Discovery Interview

## Purpose

This interview will define what the Merkhet Ventures website needs to say, who it is for, and what it should invite people to do. The goal is not to manufacture a conventional company story. It is to make the existing point of view clear, credible, and useful to someone who encounters a Merkhet Ventures email address or the website for the first time.

Answers may be rough notes, examples, fragments, or stories. We will refine the language later.

## Working copy already agreed

### Vision

> A future where knowledge, wisdom, and technology are woven into everyday life, helping individuals, families, and communities live in harmony with themselves, one another, and the environment.

### Mission

> Merkhet Ventures uses social entrepreneurship and creative technologies to bring good knowledge and wisdom into practice—developing ventures, tools, and systems that make better ways of living more accessible.

### About

> Merkhet Ventures explores how economic activity can exist in greater harmony with people, communities, and the living world.

**Notes / answer:**

In three years, Merkhet Ventures could become an operating hub for John's work: a platform with support capacity, potentially including an analyst or similar role, to help manage projects, initiatives, partnerships, and operations.

The work would move through partners, social entrepreneurs, and their own vehicles, creating a network of aligned initiatives and relationships rather than a single centralized organization doing everything itself.

An important long-term possibility is responsible capital management: impact funds generated by Merkhet or entrusted to it for management, including grants, philanthropic funds, or other capital dedicated to impact initiatives. This is an aspiration and strategic horizon, not a current public claim.

The main perception to avoid is a vague or “fluffy” consulting organization: one that uses broad impact language without real understanding, operational depth, or a path to practical work.

The site should therefore communicate grounded experience, disciplined thinking, and a credible movement from strategy into implementation—without overstating Merkhet's current portfolio or making unsupported claims.

Specific impressions to avoid:

- A generic AI, digital marketing, or growth agency.
- An investment fund that only supplies capital.
- A government contractor or policy lobby.
- A nonprofit or activist organization.
- A large, established venture firm with a portfolio and team that do not yet exist.
- A personal blog with big ideas but no practical route into action.
- An umbrella so broad that it appears unfocused.

Do not name current or past ventures publicly at this stage. There are several, with different relationships to Merkhet, and a public list could be confusing, incomplete, or imply a unified portfolio that does not yet need to be presented.

Nexus Marketplace and the work with the New Zealand government have relevance to the story, including a connection that now flows into So One Good Seed, but they should remain background context for now rather than featured public case studies.

The site can stand on Merkhet's point of view, strategic role, and invitation to collaborate. A carefully curated “selected work,” “ventures,” or Labs section can be added later when the relationships, names, and public narrative are ready.

Creative technology should increase connection and improve communication. Its deeper purpose is to improve shared understanding, so that people and organizations can build consensus and make decisions that take more people into account.

Technology can also accelerate the spread of ideas and solutions, while giving individuals, companies, organizations, and communities more capacity and capability to do their work. It should augment existing skills and knowledge rather than displace human judgment or connection.

For Merkhet, AI and digital tools are therefore not ends in themselves. They are enabling infrastructure for communication, understanding, inclusion, decision-making, learning, and implementation.

Merkhet's social-entrepreneurship purview is broad but coherent: it is interested in systems-level work that helps people, communities, and institutions become more capable, equitable, and resilient.

Areas of interest currently include:

- **Community wealth and ownership:** cooperatives, community trusts, local investment vehicles, workforce initiatives, and small-business initiatives.
- **Civic and governance innovation:** better ways for communities and institutions to make decisions, work together, and build trust.
- **Infrastructure for impact organizations:** practical systems such as grant management, funding workflows, and organizational capability.
- **Human and community development:** health, social services, education, wellbeing, and the conditions that help people and communities thrive.
- **Responsible transition:** helping communities move through economic, energy, or environmental change without being left behind.
- **Technology in service of impact:** including AI and digital tools that make social organizations and communities more capable.
- **Venture and strategy support:** helping mission-led founders and organizations move from a promising idea to an operating initiative.

This breadth is informed by John's work with the New Zealand government on all-of-government change. That experience opened a view across the public-services and social sectors, including health, social services, and education, and included work on a strategy to bring more funding to mental health for Māori and Pacific youth.

The United Nations Sustainable Development Goals resonated as a useful picture of the range of work Merkhet could engage with. They are context for the scale and interconnectedness of the work, rather than a claim that Merkhet currently addresses every goal.

Harmony is not an unrealistic claim that every choice has no downside. Change involves trade-offs, and those trade-offs need to be understood across time.

The goal is to strengthen people, communities, and the environment over the long term. In a transitional world, an immediate hard stop can itself create harm: for example, ending an existing system before people and communities have a viable alternative may weaken them in the short term, even when the long-term environmental goal is right.

Merkhet's approach is to handle transitions with care and honesty. Sometimes difficult transitions are necessary, but the work is to make them easier, simpler, safer, and less harmful where possible—balancing the near-term realities people face with the longer-term direction of change.

Good knowledge is not merely correct, useful, or profitable. It is knowledge joined with wisdom: knowledge that genuinely helps a person, community, or system see a problem differently, make a better decision, or find a path toward a solution.

Impact should not be overstated as an immediate physical or measurable change in the world. It often begins earlier, through mindset, philosophy, values, and beliefs. A meaningful shift in perspective can be the beginning of impact; it germinates, grows, and may later become practical implementation and visible work.

Merkhet's contribution often begins at this earliest stage: helping people think differently and more clearly about the problems they are facing, while remaining oriented toward real-world benefit for people, communities, and systems.

Merkhet's primary role today is advisory and strategy. John begins by consulting: understanding the situation, providing ideas, shaping strategy, and identifying practical ways forward.

When a piece of work is ready to move from strategy into execution, Merkhet can connect it with the most relevant organization or venture. This creates a clear sequence: advisory and strategy first; delivery through the appropriate platform when appropriate.

Examples currently mentioned:

- **So One Good Seed:** a platform where Merkhet may collaborate on or support impact initiatives.
- **Grant management system:** a separate venture co-owned with a business partner, outside Merkhet Ventures.
- **AI enablement and digital marketing / growth agency:** a venture being built or developed, potentially suitable for work requiring that capability.

The website should not present all of these as one unified portfolio until their relationships and public status are confirmed. It can, however, make clear that Merkhet brings strategic work to the right platform for implementation.

Merkhet Ventures is currently a holding company and venture company. It is the umbrella used to establish other companies needed to carry out John's work.

It is also the public identity John uses when introducing himself: the domain and website may be the first thing people encounter after a conversation or email. The site is not yet being actively used as a platform, but it has an opportunity to become a more intentional, credible front door for the holding company, its ventures, and John's work.

The primary audience is people already positioned to make or enable change at scale: impact investors, social-impact funds, philanthropists, community trusts and funds, nonprofit leaders, social entrepreneurs, B Corporations, policymakers, and other systems-level changemakers.

Merkhet should also be a credible place for conversations with people who have resources, operating experience, and networks that can be deployed toward change—not only money, but influence, partnerships, institutions, and practical capability.

The site is not intended to market government services or seek government employment. Government and policy are relevant because of their role in systems change, and collaboration may be possible, but Merkhet's center of gravity is larger strategies for deploying change through communities, institutions, and social entrepreneurship.

An important emerging role for the website is as an ideas repository: a place to share thinking on governance, communities, systems, and impact, giving the right people a concrete reason to begin a conversation.

Merkhet Ventures is John's vehicle for exploring and pursuing the work he wants to do in the world to make an impact. Its current center of gravity is social entrepreneurship, with a particular focus on communities as a practical vehicle for change.

The underlying theory of change begins with the individual: people must first be able to live in greater harmony with themselves. From there, change can move outward through families, communities, and eventually the wider environment and systems around them. The website should make this direction of travel clear while presenting Merkhet as an active, personal platform for that work—not as a generic corporate brand or a finished portfolio.

### 3. The invitation

What kinds of conversations or opportunities would you genuinely welcome through a generic Merkhet Ventures inbox?

**Notes / answer:**

The site should use a generic Merkhet Ventures inbox rather than John's personal address. Its invitation should be open but gentle: people can explore collaboration, offer support, bring new ideas or initiatives, ask Merkhet to become involved in relevant work, or discuss engaging John as a high-level special advisor or consultant.

The site can eventually share selected ventures, initiatives, and organizations that Merkhet is involved with. It should not imply a large established portfolio before there is one. One existing initiative was mentioned as “One Good Seed” (name and public status to confirm).

There is a useful distinction between the main Merkhet Ventures site and a possible Merkhet Ventures Labs subdomain. The main site would hold the impact-oriented point of view, collaborations, and ventures. Labs could be a separate home for experimental work, including AI and other playful projects that may not be impact-oriented. The two could link to one another without forcing every experiment into the Merkhet Ventures mission.
